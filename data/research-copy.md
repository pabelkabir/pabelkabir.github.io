## Research overview

The Kabir Lab studies how molecular environment controls electronic structure, photophysics, and intermolecular association. We combine quantum chemistry, molecular dynamics, QM/MM, sampling, and automated analysis to connect microscopic interactions with measurable molecular behavior.

## 1. Molecular photophysics in complex environments

### Public-facing summary

Molecules do not absorb, emit, or react in isolation. Their behavior changes with electrostatics, hydrogen bonding, solvation, conformation, redox state, protonation, and the surrounding protein or material. We use excited-state electronic-structure calculations and multiscale simulation to determine how those local environments reshape spectra, charge-transfer pathways, and photochemical outcomes.

### Questions

- Which electronic-structure methods are reliable for a given excited-state property?
- How do protein electrostatics and point mutations tune chromophore color?
- How do redox and protonation states change flavin spectroscopy?
- How does hydrogen bonding alter vibrational and electronic signatures?
- How do molecular structure and external stimuli redistribute competing emission pathways?

### Methods

TD-DFT; multireference wave-function methods; EOM-CC-related methods; molecular dynamics; QM/MM; vibrational analysis; electrostatic spectral-tuning maps; comparison with spectroscopy.

### Evidence

2025 fluorenone paper; 2024 method benchmark; 2023 iLOV work; 2021 flavin IR work; 2019 flavin spectra, ESTM, and semiquinone papers.

## 2. Multiscale simulation and reliable computational workflows

### Public-facing summary

Complex simulations are useful only when their assumptions, inputs, sampling, and analysis are traceable. We develop and document workflows that connect electronic structure with molecular dynamics, explicit solvent, QM/MM, and free-energy analysis. The goal is reproducible scientific comparison, not automation for its own sake.

### Questions

- How can related proteins or molecular systems be modeled consistently?
- How much sampling is needed before a mechanistic interpretation is stable?
- Where do method choices introduce uncertainty?
- How can complicated setup and analysis steps be made transparent to students and collaborators?

### Methods and resources

QM/MM APEC workflow; ESTM workflow; AutoSolvate contribution; umbrella sampling and WHAM; Python and shell automation; documented validation and benchmark calculations.

### Evidence

Public repositories and the 2023/2024 flavin papers.

## 3. Interaction mapping and functional molecular design

### Public-facing summary

We investigate where molecules prefer to associate, how their environment changes those interactions, and how interaction patterns can guide molecular and materials design. Our workflows combine three-dimensional interaction maps, explicit-solvent sampling, geometry optimization, free-energy calculations, and chemical interpretation.

### Questions

- Where are the most favorable interaction regions around a molecule?
- How do solvent and molecular geometry change association pathways?
- What molecular features control binding between ionic polymers and anionic probes?
- How can interaction principles inform photoactive or ion-conducting materials?

### Methods and resources

Ab-Initio Interaction Maps; explicit-solvent molecular dynamics; umbrella sampling; potential of mean force; electronic-structure calculations; spectroscopy-informed interpretation.

### Evidence

AIIM and umbrella-sampling repositories; 2016-2018 cobaltocenium, RAFT, binding, and membrane papers; 2016 thesis.
